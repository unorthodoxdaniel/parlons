# Prompt bank expansion: second batch of 100 review drafts

> Historical draft batch. Final selection, approved rewrites and French adaptations were imported on 21 October 2026; see [the import record](prompt-bank-expansion.md). The original draft wording below is preserved for reference.

Status: English review drafts only. Not approved or imported. Ten new ideas per existing theme. Adapt approved ideas naturally into French after review.

Use the stable IDs to mark **keep**, **cut**, **rewrite**, or **more like this**. IDs continue from the first expansion batch. Unmarked drafts remain unreviewed. Personal stories may always be invented; private disclosure and specialist knowledge are not required.

Approving both expansion batches would add 200 ideas, or 400 prompt rows after French adaptation, bringing the current 306-row bank to 706.

## Everyday life (`everyday-life`)

- **EL11.** You can taste everything perfectly, but only eat meals at room temperature for a week. What would you put on the menu?
- **EL12.** A parcel arrives with your address and somebody else's name. Tell the story of what happens next.
- **EL13.** Which sound belongs in a home, and which sound would you happily never hear again?
- **EL14.** You have to explain your favourite snack to someone who cannot see it. Make them want to try it.
- **EL15.** A shop lets you try any object at home for a week before buying it. What would you test, and what would count as a success?
- **EL16.** You must prepare dinner using the ingredient you usually avoid. How would you make it appealing?
- **EL17.** What is worth doing the long way, even when a shortcut exists?
- **EL18.** Your local café asks you to name a new sandwich. Invent the sandwich and explain the name.
- **EL19.** What small repair have you been putting off—or could an imaginary character be putting off? Make the case for finally doing it today.
- **EL20.** You can keep either all your cups or all your plates. Explain how you would manage without the other.

## Relationships (`relationships`)

- **RE11.** Someone remembers your name every time, but you have forgotten theirs again. How would you handle your next meeting?
- **RE12.** Two friends always say “anything is fine” when choosing where to eat. Invent a way for them to make a decision.
- **RE13.** A friend shares good news about something you would never want for yourself. How would you celebrate with them?
- **RE14.** You accidentally spoil a surprise. Explain how you would try to save the occasion.
- **RE15.** Is keeping a small promise more meaningful than making a grand gesture? Give a situation that tests your answer.
- **RE16.** A friend wants to turn your shared hobby into a business. What would you discuss before agreeing?
- **RE17.** You meet someone whose interests are completely unfamiliar to you. What could you ask beyond “Why do you like that?”
- **RE18.** Someone you have not spoken to in years sends “I saw this and thought of you.” Invent what they sent and the conversation that follows.
- **RE19.** You and a friend enjoy an activity at very different skill levels. How could you make doing it together enjoyable for both?
- **RE20.** When does a running joke stop being funny? Describe how someone could retire it without creating an argument.

## Culture (`culture`)

- **CU11.** Which fictional villain would give surprisingly useful advice about an ordinary problem?
- **CU12.** You are designing the cover of a book that has no title yet. Describe an image that would make someone pick it up.
- **CU13.** Does a terrible ending ruin a good story? Explain what an ending owes its audience.
- **CU14.** Choose an ordinary sound and explain how it could become the opening of a piece of music.
- **CU15.** A television series you love gets one more episode, set twenty years later. What should happen in it?
- **CU16.** Would you rather see an excellent performance of something familiar or an uneven performance of something completely new?
- **CU17.** You are hosting a meal where every dish must connect to the same story. Choose a story and explain the menu.
- **CU18.** A critic calls your favourite work “pleasant but forgettable.” How would you respond without simply saying they are wrong?
- **CU19.** What makes a costume convincing when it has almost no budget?
- **CU20.** An audience is allowed to interrupt a play once and change one character's decision. When would you use that opportunity?

## Society (`society`)

- **SO11.** A town has one empty shop and can offer it rent-free for a year. What kind of project should get the space?
- **SO12.** Should a queue hold a place for someone who leaves briefly? Propose a rule strangers could accept.
- **SO13.** A public clock has shown the wrong time for years and become a local landmark. Should it be fixed?
- **SO14.** A popular viewing spot is becoming damaged by visitors. How could people keep enjoying it while helping it recover?
- **SO15.** Design a public bench for people who want different things: conversation, rest, solitude or a quick lunch.
- **SO16.** A town can celebrate one person with a statue, or many people with a shared memorial. Which would you choose, and why?
- **SO17.** Should a community event be cancelled if only a few people sign up, even when those people are very enthusiastic?
- **SO18.** You can ask everyone in your neighbourhood one question anonymously. What would you ask, and how could the answers help?
- **SO19.** A town's unofficial nickname is more popular than its real name. Should the town adopt it?
- **SO20.** A public service wants to replace written instructions with pictures. What might become easier, and what might become harder?

## Memories (`memories`)

- **ME11.** Describe a time you were proud of something that would seem very small to somebody else. A fictional example is welcome.
- **ME12.** Tell a story in which the weather changes everyone's plans for the better.
- **ME13.** What did you once save up for? Describe the waiting as well as the moment of getting it, or invent the story of someone who did.
- **ME14.** Describe a game you played as a child, including one rule that adults might find confusing.
- **ME15.** Think of a meal you remember clearly. Explain what made the occasion memorable beyond the food.
- **ME16.** Tell a story about recognising someone in an unexpected place. You may invent the encounter.
- **ME17.** What phrase did you hear often when you were younger? Explain how its meaning might change as someone grows up.
- **ME18.** Describe a celebration through the work that happened before the guests arrived.
- **ME19.** Tell the story of something that seemed like bad luck at first but later became useful.
- **ME20.** Imagine interviewing someone about a day you both remember. What would you ask to discover something you missed?

## Learning (`learning`)

- **LE11.** You can ask an expert to explain one thing, but you cannot ask a follow-up question. How would you phrase your question?
- **LE12.** Explain how you would learn a board game if nobody available had played it before.
- **LE13.** Would you rather practise with someone slightly better than you or teach someone just starting? Explain what each could reveal.
- **LE14.** Someone gives the right answer for the wrong reason. How would you help them notice without discouraging them?
- **LE15.** You have to describe a familiar object without using its name, its colour or what it is made of. Choose an object and try.
- **LE16.** What makes an explanation satisfying even when it does not answer every question?
- **LE17.** You are allowed to change your mind halfway through a debate. What would make that feel like progress rather than defeat?
- **LE18.** Invent a way to practise directions with someone without leaving the room.
- **LE19.** A tutorial skips the step you need most. Explain how you would work out what is missing.
- **LE20.** Choose a skill you know a little about. What would you tell a beginner to ignore for their first week?

## Imagination (`imagination`)

- **IM11.** Shadows get one hour off each day. Where does yours go, and how do you know?
- **IM12.** You find a vending machine that accepts stories instead of money. What would you tell it, and what would you hope to receive?
- **IM13.** A mountain applies to move closer to the sea. Present its request to the committee responsible.
- **IM14.** One day, every mirror shows you dressed for a different possible career. What do you see, and which version interests you?
- **IM15.** You become the caretaker of a cloud for a week. Describe the instructions you receive.
- **IM16.** A hotel offers rooms with different laws of physics. Describe one room and the warning on its door.
- **IM17.** Dreams now come with receipts. What would yours list, and which item would you dispute?
- **IM18.** A travelling circus performs only ordinary tasks, exceptionally well. Describe its star act.
- **IM19.** An island appears once a year for six hours. You can take one person and one object. Plan the visit.
- **IM20.** You have been asked to write the instruction manual for luck. Explain its first three rules.

## Work (`work`)

- **WO11.** A customer asks for something you cannot provide. Explain how you would still make the conversation useful.
- **WO12.** Your team can remove one recurring report. How would you decide which one nobody would miss?
- **WO13.** You are asked to train your replacement in a single afternoon. What would you teach first, and what would you write down?
- **WO14.** A colleague takes excellent notes that nobody reads. What could make those notes more useful?
- **WO15.** Would you rather solve a difficult problem alone or an easy problem with a difficult group? Explain your choice.
- **WO16.** Your job title disappears, but your work stays the same. What title would you give yourself so a stranger understands what you do?
- **WO17.** A team wants to celebrate finishing a demanding project, but everyone is tired. Suggest a celebration people might actually welcome.
- **WO18.** Someone asks for a “quick favour” that will take half a day. How would you respond?
- **WO19.** You are choosing between a polished proposal with few details and a rough proposal with a clear plan. What would you ask before deciding?
- **WO20.** A workplace wants every employee to spend one day doing another person's job. Which swaps could be useful, and what would need preparation?

## Travel (`travel`)

- **TR11.** You arrive at a station with two hours before your connection. How would you decide whether to explore or stay nearby?
- **TR12.** A friend asks you to plan a trip but refuses to name a destination. What three questions would you ask first?
- **TR13.** You can return to a place you love, but only in its least popular season. What might you discover?
- **TR14.** Your accommodation has an unexpected house rule. Invent the rule and explain whether you would stay.
- **TR15.** Describe a destination through the things someone should bring home in their memory rather than their luggage.
- **TR16.** You are showing someone around a place you are still discovering yourself. How would you make that part of the experience?
- **TR17.** A travel companion wants to plan every meal in advance. You want to decide when you are hungry. Negotiate a compromise.
- **TR18.** You miss your stop and end up somewhere unfamiliar. Tell a story in which that becomes the best part of the day.
- **TR19.** Would you take a trip if you could not tell anyone about it afterward? Explain whether that would change your destination.
- **TR20.** You are writing a review of a place you enjoyed despite its inconveniences. What would you warn visitors about, and why would you still recommend it?

## Technology (`technology`)

- **TE11.** Your phone can display only one number on its home screen. What number would be useful without becoming a distraction?
- **TE12.** An app congratulates you every time you complete a small task. When would that be encouraging, and when would it become annoying?
- **TE13.** You must explain the difference between deleting a file and losing track of it to someone who uses only paper documents.
- **TE14.** A smart fridge refuses to let you forget food at the back. Describe how it should remind you without becoming unbearable.
- **TE15.** Would you want a tool that rewrites every message to sound more confident? Explain when you would turn it off.
- **TE16.** A website lets you choose between instant answers and slower answers with clearer explanations. Which would you use for different tasks?
- **TE17.** You can make one device work with every brand of charger, accessory or replacement part. Which device would benefit most?
- **TE18.** A family shares one tablet. How should it handle everyone's different preferences without making every use complicated?
- **TE19.** Your calendar starts declining invitations on your behalf. What would it need to understand before you trusted it?
- **TE20.** A digital assistant misunderstands a perfectly ordinary request in an amusing way. Invent the request, the result and how you would explain the mistake.
